Essay

On Gaslighting

A personal reflection

Gaslighting is not about making someone believe something.
It is about making them stop believing in themselves.

by · Sugyeong
scroll
I

I have encountered narcissists and gaslighting more times than I can count.

They set the trap with sweetness. Once you're caught, it is difficult to escape — not because the bait is irresistible, but because the trap itself is engineered to hold you.

Acquaintances, shared history, social circles, the invisible web of professional life. The moment a target tries to pull away, the gaslighter is quick to reel them back in — wearing the mask of persuasion, wearing the face of concern.

There is a saying that even a daughter-in-law who has left home will return for the right dish. When I heard it, my first thought was how much it resembles gaslighting. It is something else entirely from the carrot-and-stick dynamic. What looks like a carrot is, itself, the trap.

II

A narcissist and a gaslighter always need attendants.

They rebuild kingdoms with themselves at the center. This is not leadership. A leader grows people. A gaslighter erodes them — quietly, gradually dissolving the attendant's independence until almost nothing remains.

"Without me, you are nothing."

The problem is that it rarely sounds like a threat. In the beginning it sounds like worry. Sometimes it even sounds like love. That is what makes gaslighting so dangerous.

III

Every person is, at their core, an independent being. We are capable of standing alone — and yet we lean on one another. The problem is simply that some people choose to exploit that leaning.

After years of experiencing gaslighting in many different forms, I arrived at one conclusion: I am someone who can rise on my own.

Those who have been gaslighted for a long time often find, as time passes, that their memories begin to soften and romanticize. This, too, becomes material for exploitation. People who tend to become targets are usually high in empathy, generous toward others by nature. That is precisely why practicing the art of drawing one's own boundaries matters so much. I am still learning how.

When a rose thorn finally works its way free from your skin,
it hurts. And it leaves a hollow.

But you have to wait
until new flesh grows back.

That time belongs to healing —
and nothing else.

fin  ·  Sugyeong
